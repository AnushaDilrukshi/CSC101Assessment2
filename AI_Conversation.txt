2. Problem 1: Tip Calculator
The Scenario
Imagine you work at a restaurant in Perth.
Your manager wants a program that calculates tips for customers.
The staff will enter:
The customer's bill amount.
The tip percentage.
The program will calculate:
The tip amount.
The total bill, including the tip.
STEP 1: Understand the Problem
Your lecturer asks:
"In your own words, what is this program supposed to do?"
You need to explain the purpose of the program in 2–3 sentences.
For example:
The program helps restaurant staff calculate customer tips. Staff enter the bill amount and the tip percentage. The program calculates the tip and the total amount the customer needs to pay.
Try to express this in your own words.
STEP 2: Identify Inputs and Outputs
First, understand these two words.
Input: Information entered into a program.
Output: Information produced by a program.
For the tip calculator:
Inputs
Outputs
Bill amount
Tip amount
Tip percentage
Total bill
Numbers
Money values in dollars
For example:
Input:
Bill = $45.80
Tip = 15%
Output:
Tip amount = $6.87
Total bill = $52.67
STEP 3: Work It By Hand
Your lecturer gives you:
Bill amount = $45.80
Tip percentage = 15%
You must calculate the answer manually.
Calculation 1: Find the tip amount.
Formula:
Tip = Bill × Tip percentage ÷ 100
= 45.80 × 15 ÷ 100
= $6.87
Calculation 2: Find the total bill.
Total = Bill + Tip
= 45.80 + 6.87
= $52.67
Final result:
The tip is $6.87, and the customer must pay $52.67.
This is how you demonstrate your calculations to the lecturer.
3. Problem 2: Late Fee Calculator
The Scenario
Imagine you work at a library in Perth.
The library allows customers to borrow books for 14 days.
If someone returns a book late, they must pay a fine.
The library charges:
$0.50 for every overdue day.
However, the maximum fine is $10.00.
This means the fine cannot exceed $10, regardless of how many days the book is overdue.
STEP 1: Understand the Problem
Your lecturer asks:
"What is this program supposed to do?"
You need to explain its purpose in 2–3 sentences.
For example:
The program calculates library fines for overdue books. The librarian enters the number of days a book is overdue. The program calculates the fine, but the maximum fine is $10.
STEP 2: Identify Inputs and Outputs
Input
Output
Number of overdue days
Fine amount
Whole number
Amount in dollars
Special cases:
If the book is not overdue, the fine is $0.
If the calculated fine exceeds $10, the maximum fine is $10.
STEP 3: Work It By Hand
Your lecturer gives you three cases.
You must calculate all three.
Case A: Book is 5 days late
Fine = Overdue days × $0.50
= 5 × 0.50
= $2.50
Answer: The fine is $2.50.
Case B: Book is 25 days late
Fine = 25 × 0.50
= $12.50
However, the library has a maximum fine of $10.
Therefore:
Final fine = $10.00
You should explain why the fine is $10 instead of $12.50.
Case C: Book is 0 days late
Fine = 0 × 0.50
= $0.00
Answer: No fine is required because the book is not overdue.
